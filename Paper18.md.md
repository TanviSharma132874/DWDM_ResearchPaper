PAPER 18 | SYSTEMATIC REVIEW (PRISMA)
Environmental air pollution and chronic rhinosinusitis: A systematic review
Evelyn M. Leland, Varun Vohra, Stella M. Seal, Z. Zhang, M. Ramanathan Jr.
Laryngoscope Investigative Otolaryngology, 2022; 7(2): 349–360
Links: DOI | Consensus summary
## 1. Background and objective
Lab and animal work suggested air pollutants can harm the nose and sinuses, but human evidence had not been gathered systematically.
Objective: assess how air pollution affects CRS pathogenesis, severity and progression, without the confusion of workplace exposures.
It is described as the first systematic review focused on EPA criteria air pollutants and CRS disease markers.
## 2. Dataset used
No patient dataset. The data are published human studies found by a structured database search.

## 3. Methodology flowchart

Figure 1. PRISMA-style selection flow
Step by step
Search five databases in August 2021.
Screen 11,983 articles against the inclusion criteria following PRISMA.
Keep 10 human studies on EPA criteria air pollutants and CRS.
Group the evidence into incidence/prevalence, severity/quality of life, and histopathology.

## 5. What it means
Air pollution, particularly particulate matter, is correlated with CRS incidence, prevalence and severity, with evidence of tissue-level change.
The authors call for more research on how pollutant components could cause CRS and type 2 inflammation.
## 6. Limitations to mention
Only 10 studies, and three of the severity studies came from the same research group, so evidence is less independent than it looks.
Studies used different pollutants and exposure methods, which makes direct comparison hard.
Published in 2022; newer studies (including papers 17 and 19) have added evidence since.

## 8. Likely questions and short answers
Q: What is PRISMA?
A: A reporting guideline that makes systematic reviews transparent and reproducible.
Q: Why exclude occupational exposures?
A: To isolate the effect of general environmental air pollution.
Q: What are eosinophilic aggregates?
A: Clusters of eosinophils (an immune cell) in tissue, a marker of type 2 inflammation.
## 9. How it fits the team project
Bridges paper 16 (broad meta-analysis) and the two CRS-specific studies (17 and 19) in a clean evidence chain.
