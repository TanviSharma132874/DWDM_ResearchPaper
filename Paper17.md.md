PAPER 17 | PROSPECTIVE COHORT STUDY
Air pollution, genetic factors, and chronic rhinosinusitis: A prospective study in the UK Biobank
Qinfeng Zhou, J. Ma, S. Biswal, N. R. Rowan, N. R. London, C. A. Riley et al.
Science of the Total Environment, 2024; 940: 173526
Links: DOI | Consensus summary
## 1. Background and objective
CRS comes in two main forms: with nasal polyps (CRSwNP) and without (CRSsNP).
Earlier work linked air pollution to CRS, but nobody had systematically tested how air pollution and genetic susceptibility act together.
Hypothesis: more air pollution raises CRS risk, and genetic susceptibility may change that effect.
## 2. Dataset used
Dataset: the UK Biobank, one of the largest population studies in the world.

## 3. Methodology flowchart

Figure 1. Study design and analysis flow
Step by step
Start from UK Biobank participants and select eligible adults (367,298).
Assign each person air-pollution exposure at their home address (land-use regression).
Calculate each person’s genetic risk with a polygenic risk score.
Follow participants for new CRS, CRSwNP and CRSsNP cases.
Use Cox proportional-hazards models for each outcome and test for interaction between pollution and genetics.

## 5. What it means
Both environment and genes matter, and they appear to contribute independently rather than amplifying each other.
Reducing exposure is relevant for everyone, including people with high genetic risk.
## 6. Limitations to mention
Pollution is estimated at the home address, not measured on the person, so some exposure is misclassified.
Absence of statistical interaction does not rule out a biological interaction.
Cohort volunteers may not represent the whole population; results are associations, not proof of cause.

## 8. Likely questions and short answers
Q: What is a polygenic risk score?
A: A single number that adds up the small effects of many genetic variants to estimate inherited risk.
Q: Why Cox models?
A: They compare the rate of new disease over time between exposure groups.
Q: Why does “no interaction” matter?
A: It means pollution did not make high-genetic-risk people disproportionately worse off in this data.
