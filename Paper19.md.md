PAPER 19 | COHORT STUDY (LEVEL OF EVIDENCE 4)
Air Pollution Exposure Affects Severity and Cellular Endotype of Chronic Rhinosinusitis With Nasal Polyps
Xiao-Zhe Yang, S. Shen, Y. Deng, C. Wang, L. Zhang
The Laryngoscope, 2022; 132(11): 2103–2110
Links: DOI | Consensus summary
## 1. Background and objective
CRS with nasal polyps (CRSwNP) varies by cellular endotype, including an eosinophilic form (ECRSwNP).
Air pollution is a recognised environmental risk factor for CRS progression.
Objective: test whether exposure to five pollutant types relates to CRSwNP severity and to its cellular endotype.
## 2. Dataset used
Dataset: clinical records of CRSwNP patients in Beijing, joined to public air-quality monitoring data.

## 3. Methodology flowchart

Figure 1. Study workflow
Step by step
Enrol CRSwNP patients (n = 282) and map their home locations.
Estimate each patient’s exposure from the nearest Beijing monitoring data.
Record severity: CT and endoscopy scores, VAS symptoms, nasal patency and airflow resistance.
Correlate exposure with severity and compare groups with different exposure types.
Run logistic regression to find pollution risk factors for the eosinophilic endotype.

## 5. What it means
PM2.5 is a possible risk factor for the eosinophilic endotype, which suggests pollution may help shape the type of inflammation, not just its amount.
Short-term exposure did not move nasal airflow measures, so long-term exposure is likely what matters.
## 6. Limitations to mention
Single city, 282 patients; Level 4 evidence means it cannot prove cause.
Exposure comes from fixed monitoring sites, not personal monitors.
The odds ratio of 1.047 is small per unit; clinical meaning depends on the real range of PM2.5 exposure.

## 8. Likely questions and short answers
Q: What is an endotype?
A: A subtype defined by the underlying biology (here, eosinophil-dominant inflammation) rather than symptoms alone.
Q: How was exposure measured?
A: From Beijing monitoring-site data matched to each patient’s home location.
Q: Is an OR of 1.047 important?
A: It is per unit of PM2.5, so differences of 10 or 20 units compound; but it is still an association.
## 9. How it fits the team project
Shows a concrete clinical dataset where pollution is a feature that machine-learning models could use to predict endotype.
