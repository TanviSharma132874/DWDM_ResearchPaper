## 1. Background and objective
Chronic rhinosinusitis (CRS) is a large part of everyday otolaryngology practice, so it offers many chances for AI to help with diagnosis and management.
Objective: survey every way AI has been used for CRS diagnosis or management and summarise the field in one place.
A scoping review (not a meta-analysis) is the right tool when a field is new and varied: it charts what has been done instead of pooling one effect size.
## 2. Dataset used
This paper has no patient dataset. Its “data” are the published studies themselves.

## 5. What it means
Diagnosis dominates, so the field is still mostly asking “Can AI recognise CRS?” rather than “Can AI guide treatment?”. Treatment-planning is the least developed area at 12%.
The conclusion is that AI has real potential to change how an otolaryngologist approaches CRS.
## 6. Limitations to mention
A scoping review maps the evidence but does not pool accuracy across studies, so it cannot say which model is best.
Technique labels overlap (CNNs are a form of deep learning), so the percentages should be read as category counts, not a clean split.
Paediatric and non-English studies were excluded, so the picture covers adult, English-language work only.

## 8. Likely questions and short answers
Q: What is a scoping review?
A: A review that maps how much research exists on a topic and what kinds, without pooling results or ranking quality.
Q: Why only 49 of 573?
A: Most records did not use AI for adult CRS diagnosis or management, or fell into an excluded group (non-English, pre-2003, acute, paediatric).
Q: Which AI model is best?
A: The review does not answer that; it reports how often each technique appears.
